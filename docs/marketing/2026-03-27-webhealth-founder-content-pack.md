## How To Use This Pack

- Treat this as a working draft, not sacred copy.
- Keep the core promise stable for at least 30 days.
- Edit wording to sound more like you, but do not drift away from the core problem:
  `traffic changed -> why did it change -> what should I fix first`
- On X, native posts should dominate. Do not attach a link to every post.
- On Reddit, post useful text first. Link only if the thread rules allow it and the link genuinely helps.

## Voice Guide

Use a mixed voice:

- `50%` serious operator
- `30%` founder build-in-public
- `20%` casual/direct

Writing rules:

- Sound like a founder who actually looks at SEO data, not a generic marketer.
- Lead with an observation, frustration, or pattern you noticed.
- Prefer short statements over long explanations.
- Use specifics like `clicks`, `pages`, `CTR`, `deploy`, `crawl`, `affected URLs`, `period-over-period`.
- Avoid hype like `game-changing`, `revolutionary`, `10x`, `crush your SEO`.
- Avoid fake certainty. Use `usually`, `often`, `my guess`, `what I keep seeing` when needed.
- Keep CTAs understated: `demo here`, `sample report here`, `I built this because...`

## Posting Playbook

### X

- Post once per day for 30 days.
- Keep about `70%` of posts native with no link.
- Use links on roughly `2` days per week.
- Reply to `5-10` relevant posts per day from SEOs, indie founders, developers, and SaaS builders.
- Spend more time replying than drafting.

Reply targets:

- people talking about traffic drops
- people talking about content updates or deploy regressions
- founders doing SEO themselves
- developers shipping marketing pages and docs
- freelancers talking about client reporting pain

### Reddit

- Start by commenting helpfully for a few days before posting.
- Prefer text posts over launch posts.
- Do not paste the same draft into multiple subreddits.
- Check each community's rules on the day you post.
- Reddit's official spam guidance is still strict about repeated or self-interested promotion, so keep your contribution ratio healthy and your links rare.

Candidate communities to evaluate before posting:

- `r/SEO`
- `r/TechSEO`
- `r/sideproject`
- `r/indiehackers`
- niche founder or dev communities where text-based case studies are normal

Avoid using broad business communities as the main channel if they are hostile to self-promo.

## Awareness Tactics Beyond Posting

If you want people to become aware of WebHealth faster, do these alongside the posts:

- reply under founders talking about traffic or SEO pain every day
- post screenshot-based answers instead of only standalone posts
- turn the best X posts into short LinkedIn posts or Indie Hackers posts
- ask early users or friends for one sentence of proof after they try the demo
- collect objections and confusing phrases from replies, then reuse that language on the site
- keep the CTA stable for a month so people do not have to re-learn what to click

## What Not To Do

- do not post `I built an SEO tool` every few days
- do not over-explain the architecture in public content
- do not lead with `AI` unless it supports a more concrete benefit
- do not stuff every post with a link
- do not pitch Reddit communities before you have commented there
- do not try to sound like a brand account

## Editing Notes For Future Feedback

When you get reactions, capture them in four buckets:

- `clear`: people immediately get the value
- `confused`: people do not understand what WebHealth actually does
- `interesting`: people like the idea but do not feel the pain strongly
- `strong`: people say they need this or would use this

Then tighten future posts around the `strong` and `confused` buckets first.
